TackRoom privacy policy

Last updated: November 24, 2026

TackRoom is a phone and iPad companion to the TackRoom extension for Visual Studio Code. This policy covers the TackRoom mobile app for iOS, iPadOS and Android.

The short version

TackRoom has no account and no advertising, and we never sell or share data. Your chats, files and projects never leave the path between your phone and your own computer. The app does use Google Firebase for three things: crash reports, anonymous usage counts, and push notifications. You can turn crash reports and usage counts off in Settings > Share diagnostics.

What the app handles, and where it stays

  • Your computer's chats, files and settings. The app talks only to the TackRoom extension on a computer you paired yourself. What it shows (sessions, messages, diffs, files you open, your VS Code theme) comes from that computer and is kept in the app's memory. An optional on-device chat cache is off by default; when you turn it on, it stays on your device and is deleted when you remove the computer.
  • Pairing keys. When you pair, the app makes a key pair on your device. The private key is kept in the device's secure storage (Keychain on iOS, Keystore on Android) and never leaves it. The app also keeps the computer's name, public key and addresses, so it can reconnect.
  • Camera and photos. The camera scans the pairing QR code shown in VS Code, and takes a photo when you tap Camera in a chat's Add context sheet. On iPhone and iPad, that sheet shows your recent photos once you allow photo access; on Android, photos are chosen in the system photo picker, which gives the app only the photos you pick. A photo you attach is scaled down and sent, end-to-end encrypted, only to your own computer, with that message. Nothing is uploaded anywhere else, and the app never saves to your library.
  • Notifications. While the app is connected, notifications are raised by the app itself from messages your computer sends. The app also registers with Firebase Cloud Messaging (through Apple's push service on iPhone and iPad), which gives it a device push token; that token is used only to deliver notifications to this device.
  • Crash reports and usage counts (Firebase Crashlytics and Google Analytics for Firebase). When the app crashes, a crash report is sent with the stack trace, device model, OS version and app version. Analytics records which screens are opened and app launches, tied to a random app-instance identifier, not to you. Neither includes your chats, prompts, files, file names, project names or computer names, and the app does not use the advertising identifier. Turn both off in Settings > Share diagnostics. Google's handling of this data is covered by the Firebase privacy terms.
  • The demo. "Try the demo" shows made-up sample data built into the app. Nothing you do in the demo is sent anywhere.

How the connection works

Every message between the app and your computer is end-to-end encrypted with keys only your phone and your computer hold. When the computer uses a Microsoft Dev Tunnel as a relay, the relay carries only encrypted data it cannot read. On the same Wi-Fi network the phone connects to the computer directly. Microsoft's handling of Dev Tunnels traffic is covered by the Microsoft Privacy Statement.

The coding agent and AI model providers you use run on your computer, under the settings you chose there. The app does not contact them.

Deleting your data

Tap Remove computer in the app to delete that computer's keys and any cached chats from your device, or revoke the phone in VS Code (TackRoom: Paired Phones). Deleting the app deletes everything it stored.

Children

TackRoom is a developer tool and is not directed at children.
